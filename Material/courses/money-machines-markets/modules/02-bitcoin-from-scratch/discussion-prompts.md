# Module 2 — Discussion Prompts

Three threaded prompts. Pick at least one to start a thread on, and reply substantively to at least two threads started by others. Quality matters, volume doesn't. See `rubrics/discussion-quality.json` for what "substantive" means here.

---

## Prompt 1 — Was Satoshi a maximalist?

Lesson 1 makes the case that the white paper is much narrower than the cultural mythology around Bitcoin suggests. Nine pages, no monetary policy argument, no "fix the money fix the world," and the 21 million cap mentioned almost in passing. Most of the politics — the gold-replacement story, the fix-the-world story, the separation-of-money-and-state story — was layered on later by users and developers, not Satoshi.

**Question for the thread:** Does it matter that the layering came later? Is "Bitcoin as Satoshi described it" a meaningfully different object from "Bitcoin as it is understood today," or is the cultural overlay an organic extension of properties that were latent in the original design? Steel-man the side that says the overlay is *necessary* (the system would not have survived without the meaning-making) and the side that says the overlay is *parasitic* (it adds claims the engineering does not actually support).

If you find yourself defending one side without effort, you have probably not steel-manned the other one well enough yet. Try.

---

## Prompt 2 — The security budget problem, on the record

Lesson 4 explains the security budget problem: as the block subsidy halves toward zero, miner revenue must increasingly come from transaction fees, and the network has never operated for any extended period in a fee-dominant regime. Most maximalist writing waves this away. Most critics write as if it's a death sentence. Both are too easy.

**Question for the thread:** Make a serious, specific prediction about what will happen to Bitcoin's security model over the next 20 years. You can pick optimistic ("fees will scale with value secured, miner revenue will be fine, here's the mechanism"), pessimistic ("hash rate will fall, attacks will become economically viable, here's the failure mode"), or transitional ("the community will eventually accept tail emission, here's how that political fight plays out"). Whichever you pick, you have to commit to *what would falsify your prediction*. If you can't say what would change your mind, you don't actually have a prediction.

**Ground rule for this thread:** no appeals to "Bitcoin will figure it out" or "the market will solve it." Both phrases are admissions that you don't have a prediction. If that's where you genuinely are, say so explicitly.

---

## Prompt 3 — Where is your line on censorship resistance?

Lesson 6 ends with the observation that censorship resistance is a *general* property — the same protocol that protects a dissident also protects an actor you consider abhorrent. There is no version of the protocol that lets you keep the protection for the cases you like while removing it for the cases you don't.

**Question for the thread:** Pick one specific real-world case (recent or historical) where you would have wanted Bitcoin's censorship resistance to *fail*. Describe the case briefly. Then write the strongest argument you can for why, despite that case, you would still defend the property in general — *or*, if you cannot write that argument honestly, explain what changes to the protocol you would actually want, and what those changes would cost.

The point of this prompt is not to get to a consensus position. The point is to make you sit with the trade-off rather than reciting it. Several different defensible answers are possible. The undefensible answer is "I want the property to apply when it suits me and not when it doesn't, and I refuse to engage with the structural reason that's not an option."

**Ground rule for this thread:** if you reply to someone whose case is the political opposite of yours, *steel-man their case before disagreeing*. The thread should not be a culture-war proxy.

## How to participate well

- Quote the specific thing you are responding to before responding to it.
- If you are about to type a sentence that contains a slogan ("number go up," "fiat is theft," "Bitcoin is a Ponzi," "have fun staying poor"), stop and rewrite it without the slogan. The thread is moderated against sloganeering and your post will be flagged.
- It is fine and good to change your mind in public. The discussion-quality rubric explicitly rewards this.
- It is fine to say "I don't know yet." It is not fine to perform certainty you don't feel.
- If you find that all three prompts are easy for you, the module has not yet done its job. The right reaction to this module is *productive discomfort*, not validation of whatever you came in believing.
