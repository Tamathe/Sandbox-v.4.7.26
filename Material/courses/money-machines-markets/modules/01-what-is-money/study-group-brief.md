# Module 1 — Study Group: The 2% Debate

## Format

Pairs. Each pair takes opposite sides of the debate below. You will switch sides halfway through. The point is to inhabit both positions, not to "win."

## The motion

> **"A central bank inflation target above zero is a quiet tax on savers and should be abolished."**

## Structure (60 minutes total)

1. **Prep (10 min)** — Each person reads the motion and silently writes down the three strongest arguments for the side they have been assigned. No talking.
2. **First round (15 min)** — Person A argues *for* the motion. Person B argues *against*. Each person gets ~6 minutes uninterrupted, then 3 minutes for direct exchange.
3. **Switch (1 min)** — Stand up, switch chairs, switch sides. This is not optional. The whole point of the exercise is to argue the side you didn't pick.
4. **Second round (15 min)** — Same format, opposite sides.
5. **Joint reflection (15 min)** — Together, write a single paragraph (3–5 sentences) answering: *what is the best argument we found on each side, and which one moved either of us the most?*
6. **Submit (4 min)** — Post the joint paragraph to the module discussion thread, tagged `#study-group-mod1`.

## Rules

- No phones except for one timer per pair.
- No looking up sources during the debate. Use what is in your head from the lessons. The point is to find out where your understanding is shallow.
- When you switch sides, you are *not* allowed to pull your punches. You have to make the new side's strongest case in good faith. Performative dunking is the worst possible failure mode here.
- If at the end of the second round you have updated your view of the motion in *either* direction, say so in the joint reflection. Updating in public is the most valuable thing that can come out of this.

## Why this format

Most of us, when we encounter a politically loaded question like central bank policy, slot ourselves onto a team within the first ten seconds and stop thinking. The forced-side-switch debate is a classic technique for short-circuiting that. You will be surprised how often the side you were "against" has arguments you had not actually considered, simply because you had not been forced to look for them. That is the only way to learn anything in this domain. Tribes do not learn. Only individuals do.
