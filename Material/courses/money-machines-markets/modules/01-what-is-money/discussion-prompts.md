# Module 1 — Discussion Prompts

Three threaded prompts. Pick at least one to start a thread on, and reply substantively to at least two threads started by others. Quality matters, volume doesn't. See `rubrics/discussion-quality.json` for what "substantive" means here.

---

## Prompt 1 — The barter myth

Lesson 1 challenges the "barter → money" story that almost everyone is taught. Anthropologists like David Graeber argue that pre-monetary societies ran mostly on credit and obligation, not on direct barter. Some economists push back hard on this.

**Question for the thread:** Does it actually matter whether the textbook origin story of money is wrong? If the conclusion (we ended up with money, money is useful) is correct, does the path we took to get there change anything about how we should think about money today?

Steel-man both sides. The thread will get repetitive if everyone agrees.

---

## Prompt 2 — Your three functions

Lesson 2 makes the case that the three functions of money (medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account) often live in different objects, especially in countries where the local currency is weak.

**Question for the thread:** In *your* life, right now, name the three different objects you use for the three different functions. Be honest. Most people in stable-currency countries will say "the dollar, the dollar, and the dollar" — and that's a fine answer if it's actually true for you. But sit with it. Are you really *storing value* in dollars, or are you doing something else (a 401k, real estate, an index fund, crypto) and just thinking of it as "saving in dollars"?

If your three functions are not the same object, what does that tell you about the local currency you live with?

---

## Prompt 3 — The 2% question

Lesson 4 ends with: *the Fed targets 2% inflation, which compounds to about a 45% loss of purchasing power over 30 years. Why is the right target 2% and not 0%?*

**Question for the thread:** Make the strongest possible case for a 2% target. Then make the strongest possible case for a 0% target. Then say which one you actually agree with and why.

The point of this prompt is not to get to consensus. The point is to notice that both cases are stronger than you probably thought, and that the answer "2% is just obviously right because deflation is bad" is doing a lot of unexamined work.

**Ground rule for this thread:** if you assert that "deflation is bad," you have to *say what is bad about it* in your own words, not just use the phrase as a magic word. Same goes for "inflation is theft."

---

## How to participate well

- Quote the specific thing you are responding to before responding to it.
- If you are about to type a sentence that contains a slogan ("fiat is theft," "Bitcoin is a Ponzi," "gold is a barbarous relic"), stop and rewrite it without the slogan.
- It is fine and good to change your mind in public. The discussion-quality rubric explicitly rewards this.
- It is fine to say "I don't know yet." It is not fine to perform certainty you don't feel.
