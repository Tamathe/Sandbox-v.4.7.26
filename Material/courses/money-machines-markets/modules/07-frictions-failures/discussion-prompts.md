# Module 7 — Discussion Prompts

Three threaded prompts. Pick at least one to start a thread on, and reply substantively to at least two threads started by others. Quality matters, volume doesn't. See `rubrics/discussion-quality.json` for what "substantive" means here.

This is the honest module. The discussion threads are graded slightly harder than other modules' threads — specifically, replies that perform certainty without earning it will be flagged. Use the vocabulary of Lesson 6 (calibration, base rate, Knightian) where it actually fits.

---

## Prompt 1 — The asymmetric standard

Most people, when evaluating Bitcoin and AI, apply two different standards. They demand the *positive* case for the technology they distrust ("show me one real use case") and accept the *narrative* case for the technology they like ("of course it's going to be transformative"). Lesson 5 (energy) is the cleanest example, but it shows up everywhere.

**Question for the thread:** Identify *your own* asymmetric standard — the place where you are demanding a higher bar of evidence from one of these technologies than the other. Be specific. Don't just say "I'm probably biased toward X" — name the concrete claim you accept on faith for the side you like and the concrete claim you demand evidence for on the side you don't. Then ask: what would it take to apply the same standard to both?

If you reply to this thread, **do not** point out other people's asymmetries. Only point out your own. The thread is useless if it becomes a finger-pointing exercise.

## Prompt 2 — The bezzle you're inside

Lesson 1 closed with an uncomfortable question about the platform you currently hold the most assets on. Use the thread to do a *partial* version of that exercise in public — partial because nobody should be doxxing their full portfolio.

**Question for the thread:** Without naming the platform, describe (a) what *kind* of platform it is (centralized exchange, DeFi protocol, brokerage, bank, fintech app, self-custody hardware), (b) what you believe about how it holds your assets, and (c) the specific gap in your knowledge that, if it turned out to be wrong in a particular way, would cost you. Then say what would have to be true for you to actually close that gap (read the prospectus, move to self-custody, diversify across platforms, accept the gap as a known cost).

Read the other replies in the thread. If you notice that several people are describing the same kind of gap, that's interesting. If you notice that nobody is, that's *also* interesting.

**Ground rule:** if you are about to write "I trust [platform name] because they have [generic safety feature]," delete it and try again. The point of the prompt is to find the part of your trust that you cannot defend, not the part you can.

## Prompt 3 — The thing you cannot be talked out of

Pick one belief from this course — any module, any lesson — that you currently hold and that you cannot imagine being talked out of, even if you encountered a strong counter-argument tomorrow.

**Question for the thread:** Name the belief in one sentence. Then, without arguing for it, write one paragraph explaining what the belief would have to *predict* that turned out to be false in order for you to update. If you cannot articulate any falsification condition, the belief is not a belief in the sense the course has been using the word — it is a commitment, and that's a different category.

Then sit with this: most of what we call our "views" in domains like this are commitments dressed up as beliefs. There is nothing wrong with having commitments. There is something wrong with mistaking them for beliefs, because commitments don't update on evidence and beliefs do, and confusing the two is how smart people get stuck.

**Ground rule for this thread:** if you reply to someone else's belief, your reply must include the words "the way I would test that is..." or something equivalent. Replies that just say "I disagree" or "I agree" will be flagged as low-quality.

## How to participate well

- Quote the specific thing you are responding to before responding to it.
- The honest module rewards saying "I don't know" precisely. It does not reward saying it as a generic disclaimer.
- It is fine to update in public. The discussion-quality rubric explicitly rewards this. It is *especially* welcome in this module.
- If you are about to use the words "obviously" or "clearly" or "it is well known that," stop and rewrite the sentence without them. In this module those words are usually a sign that the sentence is doing less work than it appears to.
