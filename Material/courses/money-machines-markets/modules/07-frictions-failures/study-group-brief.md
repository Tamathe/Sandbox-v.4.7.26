# Module 7 — Study Group: The Pre-Mortem

## Format

Groups of 3–5. One scribe. No phones except for one timer. The exercise is a *pre-mortem*: a structured technique borrowed from decision research in which a group imagines that a project has already failed and works backward to figure out why.

## The premise

> **It is April 2036. Ten years from now. Both Bitcoin and frontier AI have, in the considered judgment of most thoughtful people, turned out to be far less consequential than their 2026 advocates predicted. Neither has collapsed; both still exist; but neither reshaped the economy, the monetary system, or daily life the way the most-cited 2026 essays said they would. People look back on the 2020s the way we now look back on early-2000s nanotechnology hype: not wrong about everything, but wrong about almost everything that mattered.**
>
> **Your job is to write the autopsy.**

You are not being asked to predict whether this happens. You are being asked, as a thought experiment, to assume it has happened and work backward to the most plausible explanation.

## Structure (60 minutes total)

1. **Silent prep (8 min)** — Each person, alone, writes down three plausible reasons the 2036 outcome could have happened. One of the three must be a reason that *would have surprised them in 2026* — not a generic "it was overhyped" but a specific mechanism. No talking, no looking up sources.
2. **Round 1: Bitcoin autopsy (15 min)** — Group discussion. The scribe lists every distinct mechanism proposed for why Bitcoin under-delivered. Cluster duplicates. The group then ranks the top three by *how hard it would be to refute the mechanism today*.
3. **Round 2: AI autopsy (15 min)** — Same format, for frontier AI. Again, rank the top three by how hard they are to refute now.
4. **Cross-check (10 min)** — Compare the two lists. Are there shared root causes (regulatory capture, capability plateau, the bezzle, contextual integrity backlash, energy political constraints)? Are there mechanisms that are unique to one or the other? Notice which mechanisms made it onto both lists — those are the structural ones.
5. **The flip (8 min)** — Each person writes a single sentence: *"After this exercise, the part of my own current view that I now hold less confidently is..."* — and reads it out loud. No commentary, no defense, just the sentence.
6. **Submit (4 min)** — The scribe posts the two ranked lists plus the six "flip" sentences (one per person) to the module discussion thread, tagged `#study-group-mod7`.

## Rules

- The pre-mortem is not a debate. Nobody is defending a position. The job is to *generate* mechanisms, not to argue them down.
- "It was a Ponzi" and "it was a hype cycle" are not mechanisms. They are labels. If someone offers one of those, the group's job is to ask "by what specific path?" until they get to a mechanism with moving parts.
- The flip sentence at the end is mandatory. If you cannot honestly write one, write the sentence "I went into this exercise unable to imagine being wrong, and I am leaving it the same way" — and submit *that*. Naming the inability is more honest than faking the update.
- No social deference. The most senior person in the room does not speak first in any round.

## Why this format

The pre-mortem is one of the few group techniques that reliably produces useful skepticism without devolving into either consensus-seeking or tribal posturing. The reason it works is that it gives everyone permission to imagine the failure scenario without committing to it as a forecast. People who would never publicly say "Bitcoin might not matter" can comfortably write down three reasons it might not, because the exercise is hypothetical.

This module is the honest one. The honest move at the end of it is not to weaken your conviction but to *locate* it more precisely — to know which parts of your current view would survive a 2036 autopsy and which parts would not. The flip sentence is there to make the locating concrete. Writing it down and reading it out loud is uncomfortable. That is the point. The discomfort is the learning.
