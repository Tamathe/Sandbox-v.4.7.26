# Module 5 — Study Group: The Pitch and the Tear-Down

## Format

Groups of three. One person plays Founder, one plays Believer VC, one plays Skeptical VC. You will rotate roles so that everyone plays each role once. The point is to inhabit all three perspectives, not to "win."

## The setup

Each Founder brings a one-sentence pitch for an AI+crypto product. It can be a real product (yours, someone else's) or a deliberately invented one. The pitch must include both an LLM/AI component and a blockchain component. That's the only constraint.

## Structure (60 minutes total)

1. **Prep (5 min)** — Each person silently writes a one-sentence pitch on an index card. Don't share yet.

2. **Round 1 — Founder A (15 min)**
   - Founder A reads their pitch out loud (30 seconds).
   - Believer VC asks two genuinely curious questions, trying to find what could make this work (4 min).
   - Skeptical VC applies the Lesson 1 test out loud: "if I replaced the chain with a Postgres database operated by your company, what specifically breaks?" — and follows up until they get a real answer or a real concession (8 min).
   - Joint verdict: which bucket (1, 2, or 3) does the group think the product is in, and why (3 min).

3. **Rotate (1 min)** — Roles shift one seat. The person who was Founder is now Skeptical VC. The person who was Believer VC is now Founder. The person who was Skeptical VC is now Believer VC.

4. **Round 2 — Founder B (15 min)** — Same structure as Round 1, with the new pitch.

5. **Rotate (1 min)** — Same shift again.

6. **Round 3 — Founder C (15 min)** — Same structure with the third pitch.

7. **Group reflection (8 min)** — Together, write a single short note (3–5 sentences) answering: *of the three pitches we discussed, which one was the closest to bucket 1 or 2, and what specifically made it survive the substitution test? What did the bucket-3 pitches all have in common?*

8. **Submit (1 min)** — Post the joint reflection to the module discussion thread, tagged `#study-group-mod5`.

## Rules

- The Founder is not allowed to use the words "decentralized," "trustless," "permissionless," or "Web3" in their pitch. If those concepts are doing real work, the Founder has to say what they actually mean, in concrete terms.
- The Skeptical VC is not allowed to be sneeringly dismissive. The job is to find the actual weakness, not to perform contempt. Performative dunking is the worst possible failure mode in this exercise.
- The Believer VC is not allowed to give the Founder a free pass. The job is to find the strongest *real* case for the product, not to flatter.
- The Founder is allowed — and encouraged — to concede mid-round. Conceding well in this exercise is more valuable than defending poorly.

## Why this format

Most people in this space have spent more time pitching or being pitched than they have spent applying a rigorous test honestly. The triangle of Founder / Believer / Skeptic forces every person to do all three jobs in one hour. By the end, you should have noticed two things: (1) the substitution test from Lesson 1 is much harder to pass than it sounds, and (2) when a pitch *does* survive it, the surviving thing is usually narrower and less glamorous than the original pitch tried to make it sound. Both noticings are the point. Tribes do not learn. Triangles sometimes do.
