# Assignment — The Thesis Outline

## What this assignment is (and is not)

This is **not** the final project. The final project is a 1,500-word personal thesis and lives at `final-project/brief.md`. *Do not write it yet.*

This assignment is the scaffolding step that comes before the final project. The 1,500-word thesis is hard to write cold, and most students who try to write it cold produce something that wanders, hedges, or quietly defends a worldview they walked in with. The point of this exercise is to do the hard thinking — the *what do I actually believe and how confident am I* part — before you sit down to write the essay, so that when you do sit down, the structure is already in your head and the writing is just transcription.

You will write roughly 600 words across four short sections. None of the sections is the essay. Together they are the thesis's spine.

## What you will write

### Section 1 — Your one-sentence thesis (≈50 words including hedges)

Write the single sentence that, if a stranger read it, would let them summarize your position on the convergence of money, machines, and markets in the next ten years. *One sentence.* It can be long. It can have clauses. It cannot be more than one sentence, and it cannot be a question. It must commit.

If you find yourself writing something that could have been written by someone who had not taken this course, throw it out and start again. The sentence should *use* the course's vocabulary in a way that signals you have actually held the ideas in your hands.

### Section 2 — Your three biggest claims, each with a number (≈250 words)

Decompose your thesis into the three load-bearing claims it depends on. For each one:

- State the claim plainly.
- Attach a confidence number on a 0–100 scale.
- In one sentence, name the leading indicator that would move the number up by 5 points or down by 5 points.

This is the part of the assignment that does the most work. Almost every student, on their first attempt, discovers that one of the three claims is hiding two claims in a trench coat, or that one of the three is held at a confidence the writer cannot actually defend out loud. *That is the discovery you are paying for.* Do this section honestly and the essay will write itself; do this section dishonestly and the essay will fight you for two weeks.

### Section 3 — The strongest counter-argument (≈200 words)

Identify the smartest person who would disagree with your thesis. Name them by name if you can — a real person whose work you have read — or, if you cannot name a person, describe the *position* in enough detail that the strongest version is unmistakable. Then write the strongest version of their argument against your thesis in two or three sentences. Then write your honest response.

The grading bar here is that the steel-man has to be a steel-man. If the person you describe is too weak to be threatening, you have not found the real opposition yet. Try again.

### Section 4 — Three things that would change your mind (≈100 words)

List three specific, observable, dated things that would substantially move your thesis. Specific means *not* "if Bitcoin failed" — specific means "if global stablecoin float exceeds physical dollar circulation by 2030" or "if frontier model capability plateaus for 24 consecutive months without a new architecture." Falsifiable, observable, dated. If you cannot name three, name two and acknowledge the third is a gap. Acknowledging the gap is more valuable than faking the third.

## Format

- Plain markdown, four labeled sections, roughly 600 words total (±100).
- A one-line note at the bottom disclosing any AI tool you used and how. ("Used Claude as a sparring partner on the steel-man section." That's enough.)
- Bring the printed copy to your study group meeting (see `study-group-brief.md`).

## What I am looking for

This is graded against the **Written Thesis Rubric** (`rubrics/written-thesis.json`), the same rubric as the final project, but the bar at this stage is different: I am looking for *the bones of a defensible thesis*, not a finished essay. You can have rough phrasing. You cannot have rough thinking. I would rather see an outline that says "I believe X at 65% and I am not yet sure how to argue it" than one that says "X is obviously true" with no hedge.

The four criteria in plain language for this assignment:

1. **Clarity of claim** — One sentence in section 1 that a reader could repeat back. No mush, no slogans.
2. **Quality of reasoning** — The three claims in section 2 actually support the one sentence in section 1, and the confidence numbers are sized to the evidence you would cite if pushed.
3. **Engagement with the strongest counter-argument** — Section 3 contains a steel-man, not a strawman, and your response either holds or you note where you're updating.
4. **Voice and honesty** — The four sections sound like *you*, not like a model report. Hedges where you actually have uncertainty, commitment where you actually have conviction, and visible signs that you have wrestled with the trade-offs the course has been pushing on you.

## What will sink it

- Skipping the numbers in section 2 because they feel arbitrary. They are not arbitrary; they are the discipline. A section 2 with no numbers is a section 2 with no thinking.
- A steel-man in section 3 that any reasonable person would recognize as a strawman. Read it back to yourself in your opponent's voice. If it sounds smug, rewrite it.
- A "things that would change my mind" section in which the things are vague or undated. The whole point of section 4 is to commit to falsifiable observables.
- A thesis sentence in section 1 that could have been written by someone who had not taken this course. By module 8 you have a vocabulary the median person does not have. Use it.

## Why this assignment exists

The 1,500-word final project is the only deliverable in the course that the entire eight modules have been building toward. Sitting down to it cold is brutal. This assignment is the warmup that produces a *spine* — a one-sentence thesis, three numbered claims, a real steel-man, and three falsifiable updates — so that the essay you write next is built on something rather than discovered as you go.

When you have finished this assignment, take it into the module 8 voice session with the editor. The editor will help you sharpen the spine before you start writing the essay. The two artifacts together — the outline and the editor's notes — are the working document for the final project.

You are very nearly at the end of the course. Make this version sharp and the next version will be easier than you expect.
