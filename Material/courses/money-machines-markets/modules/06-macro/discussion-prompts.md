# Module 6 — Discussion Prompts

Three threaded prompts. Pick at least one to start a thread on, and reply substantively to at least two threads started by others. Quality matters, volume doesn't. See `rubrics/discussion-quality.json` for what "substantive" means here.

---

## Prompt 1 — Are we already in fiscal dominance?

Lesson 1 walked through the case that the United States may already be in slow-drift fiscal dominance — that the Fed's room to raise rates has been quietly constrained by the size of the federal interest bill, and that this matters even though no one has announced it. Reasonable economists disagree on whether this is true, on the threshold at which it becomes true, and on what evidence would settle the question.

**Question for the thread:** Pick one specific piece of evidence from 2023–2026 that you think *most strongly* supports the "we are in slow-drift fiscal dominance" reading, and one piece that *most strongly* supports the "no, the central bank is still independent" reading. Then say which side you find more persuasive *given your evidence* and why.

Steel-man both sides. The thread will get repetitive if everyone agrees. Anyone reaching for "MMT says deficits don't matter" or "the Fed is corrupt" gets sent back to the lesson.

---

## Prompt 2 — Energy honesty

Lesson 2 makes the claim that energy is the actual base layer of the economy and that macroeconomics systematically underweights it. This is a contested claim and there are smart economists on both sides.

**Question for the thread:** Pick the country you live in. Find (in your head, no Googling) approximately what share of its electricity comes from each major source. If you don't know within a factor of two, that itself is information. Now ask: if AI and crypto demand grows as forecast over the next decade, which specific source has to scale up to meet it, and is your country politically capable of building more of that source on the timescale required? Be specific about the political obstacle, not vague about it.

The point of this prompt is to force you to land an abstract argument on a concrete grid. Most "energy is the base layer" arguments stay safely abstract precisely because the concrete version is uncomfortable.

## Prompt 3 — The honest AI-and-jobs question

Lessons 3 and 6 sit in apparent tension. Lesson 3 says AI is going to displace a meaningful slice of cognitive labor, including the entry-level roles that train future seniors. Lesson 6 says aging societies desperately need productivity-multiplying automation to substitute for missing workers. Both are true at once.

**Question for the thread:** In your own life — your specific job, or your spouse's job, or your closest friend's job — describe one task that has *already* been substituted by AI in the last two years (be specific) and one task that has been *complemented* by AI (also specific). Then say what happened to the value of the worker holding that job: did the role get more valuable, less valuable, or just different?

**Ground rule for this thread:** if you describe a substitution, you have to also describe what the displaced time is now being spent on. "They were just laid off" is a valid answer if true, but it has to be the actually-true one, not the convenient one. Anecdotes about people you don't actually know don't count. The point is to make the abstract concrete.

## How to participate well

- Quote the specific thing you are responding to before responding to it.
- If you are about to type a sentence that contains a slogan ("AI will change everything," "the dollar is dying," "energy poverty is a myth"), stop and rewrite it without the slogan.
- It is fine and good to change your mind in public. The discussion-quality rubric explicitly rewards this.
- It is fine to say "I don't know yet" or "I haven't done the math here." It is not fine to perform certainty you don't feel.
- Reference earlier modules where appropriate. Most of the threads in this module connect to Module 1 (what money is) and Module 2 (the hard money case). You will be a better thread participant if you make those connections explicit.
