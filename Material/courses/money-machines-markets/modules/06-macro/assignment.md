# Assignment — One Country, One Decade

## The prompt

Pick **one country** — any country in the world, but a country you have some connection to or some real reason to care about — and write 800 words on how that country will most likely respond to the next decade (2026–2036) of the forces this module covers.

You must address all six of the following, in some order, integrated into your argument rather than as a checklist:

1. **Sovereign debt and fiscal posture** — Where is the country on the debt-to-GDP curve? Is it in fiscal dominance, near it, or comfortably away from it?
2. **Energy** — Where does its electricity come from? Can it scale to meet AI and electrification demand on the timescale required?
3. **Labor market and AI** — Is automation, in this country, mostly a substitute for missing workers, mostly a competitor to surviving ones, or both? Which sectors live in which regime?
4. **Capital and compute** — Does the country have a meaningful AI/compute industry, or is it a consumer of foreign-built models? What are the implications?
5. **Digital money** — Of CBDC, dollar stablecoin, and Bitcoin (and the existing banking system), which dominates retail and savings in this country in 2035, and why?
6. **Demographics** — What is the country's dependency ratio trajectory, and what is its plan (stated or unstated) for the entitlement-and-productivity gap?

You then have to make **one specific forecast** — your single most load-bearing claim about what this country looks like in 2036 — and you have to identify **the one specific assumption** that, if wrong, would cause the rest of your forecast to fall apart.

## Choose carefully

The country you pick will heavily shape how interesting the assignment is to write. Some suggestions:

- **An aging high-debt democracy** (Japan, Italy, France, UK, US): the fiscal-dominance / demographics interaction is the central drama.
- **A young indebted developing economy** (Nigeria, Pakistan, Egypt): the AI-and-jobs question lands very differently when labor is plentiful.
- **A resource-rich state with a sovereign wealth fund** (Saudi Arabia, Norway, UAE): the energy question is upside, not constraint, and the political economy is unusual.
- **A failing-currency case** (Argentina, Turkey, Lebanon, Venezuela): the digital-money question is already live and you can describe what is actually happening.
- **A tightly state-led model** (China, Singapore): all six forces interact through a much more dirigiste lens than the OECD default.
- **A small open economy** (Estonia, Ireland, Switzerland, New Zealand): you can think clearly about a system small enough to fit in your head.

The country is less interesting than the forecast. But pick one you care about, because 800 words is just enough rope to hang yourself with on a country you're faking interest in.

## What I'm looking for

Graded against the **Written Thesis Rubric** (`rubrics/written-thesis.json`). The four criteria are:

1. **Clarity of claim** — A reader should be able to summarize your single most load-bearing forecast in one sentence after reading the piece.
2. **Quality of reasoning** — Each step of your argument should follow from the previous one, and the connections between the six forces should be made explicit, not left for the reader to infer.
3. **Engagement with the strongest counter-argument** — Identify the most compelling way you could be wrong about your forecast and answer it. Not the strawman version. The real version a working economist would actually raise.
4. **Voice and honesty** — I want to hear *you* in this writeup. Not a Wikipedia summary. Specific facts about the country, specific opinions about what its political class will or won't do, and specific things you are uncertain about.

## What will sink it

- An 800-word generic essay about "the global economy" with the country's name pasted in.
- An unhedged Austrian-school doom forecast that doesn't engage with the soft-landing case.
- An unhedged techno-optimist forecast that doesn't engage with the demographic and debt math.
- A "balanced" forecast that takes no side and predicts nothing — refusing to commit is not a virtue here.
- An "AI will change everything, so my forecast is too uncertain to make" hedge — that is not a forecast, it is a refusal to engage with the assignment.
- AI-generated prose. I will recognize it. So will you when you read it back.

## Format

- 800 words (±150)
- Plain markdown
- A short bibliography at the end is welcome but optional. Numbers you cite should be roughly verifiable, not made up. Where you are guessing, say so.
- A one-line note at the end disclosing any AI tool you used and how. ("Used Claude as a sparring partner on the demographic section." That's enough.)

## Why this assignment

Module 6 brought together the threads of the course. The risk is that the threads stay in the abstract and feel like separate stories. The way to find out whether the threads have actually integrated in your head is to land them on a single country and force the integration. Forecasting a country with a coherent story across all six dimensions is harder than it looks because the dimensions are not independent — energy constrains AI, AI conditions labor, labor conditions fiscal, fiscal conditions monetary, monetary conditions digital money, demographics conditions all of them. Getting the connections right is the entire point.

You will defend this forecast in the voice session. Don't write something you can't defend out loud against a working economist who has heard every Austrian talking point twice.
