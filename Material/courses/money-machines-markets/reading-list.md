# Reading List

A running list. None of it is required. All of it is useful. Read what scratches the itch a given module raised.

## Books

- **The Bitcoin Standard** — Saifedean Ammous. Strong-form Bitcoin maximalist case. Read it for the strongest version of the argument, not for balance.
- **Broken Money** — Lyn Alden. The most readable history of how the modern monetary system actually works and why it strains under its own weight.
- **Debt: The First 5,000 Years** — David Graeber. The anthropological counterweight. Money did not start with barter, and that matters more than it sounds.
- **The Price of Tomorrow** — Jeff Booth. Deflation, technology, and why fighting it with money printing has consequences.
- **The Sovereign Individual** — Davidson & Rees-Mogg. Written in 1997, eerily prescient on some things, badly wrong on others. Read critically.
- **The Coming Wave** — Mustafa Suleyman. The containment problem for AI and synthetic biology, by someone building the thing.
- **Where Is My Flying Car?** — J. Storrs Hall. Why technological progress is not automatic and what slowed it.

## Papers

- **Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System** — Nakamoto, 2008. Nine pages. Read it three times across the course.
- **Ethereum White Paper** — Buterin, 2013.
- **Attention Is All You Need** — Vaswani et al., 2017. The transformer paper. You don't need the math to read the architecture diagram and the intuition.
- **Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models** — Kaplan et al., 2020.

## Long-form

- Lyn Alden's monthly newsletter
- Matt Levine's *Money Stuff* (Bloomberg)
- Anthropic, OpenAI, and DeepMind research blogs (read with skepticism — they are also marketing)
- Gwern's site, especially the scaling and AI essays

## Worth following critically

- Bitcoin maximalists *and* their best critics. If you only read one side, you are not learning, you are joining a team.
- Fed minutes and BIS working papers. Dry. Important. Surprisingly readable.
