# AI Use Policy

This course is partly *about* AI. It would be absurd to ban it. It would also be a waste of your time to let a model do the thinking for you. Here is the line.

## Encouraged

- **Ask a model to explain something you didn't understand in a lesson.** If a sentence in the Bitcoin white paper bounces off you, paste it into a model and ask for three different framings. This is what models are good at.
- **Use a model as a sparring partner.** Argue with it. Ask it to steel-man the other side. Ask it where your draft is weakest. The course's voice-session capstones literally use a model as a skeptical interlocutor.
- **Use a model for grunt work.** Formatting, summarizing your own notes back to you, generating practice questions, translating jargon.
- **Disclose it.** A one-line note at the end of any submission saying *"used Claude to pressure-test the argument in section 3"* is enough. Disclosure is not a confession; it is craftsmanship.

## Not allowed

- **Pasting model output as your own writing.** If a paragraph in your writeup was generated by a model, either rewrite it in your own voice or quote it as a quote. You will be able to tell when you read it back. So will a careful reader.
- **Letting a model take a mastery gate for you.** The gates are short and the explanations matter more than the score. Outsourcing them defeats the only point of taking them.
- **Submitting a final thesis you cannot defend without the model in the room.** If you couldn't reproduce the argument from memory in a conversation, it isn't yours yet.

## A note on irony

You will be using AI tools to learn about a technology that is, in part, threatening to make the kind of writing you are doing here cheap and abundant. That tension is the course. Lean into it. Notice when the model helps you think. Notice when it lets you stop thinking. Both are data.

## Acknowledgment

By acknowledging this policy you are saying: *I will use AI tools where they sharpen my thinking, I will mark the parts that are theirs, and I will not let them stand in for the part of the work that is mine to do.*
