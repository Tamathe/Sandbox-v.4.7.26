# Money, Machines, and Markets

### A course on Bitcoin, blockchains, AI, and the economy they are reshaping

---

## What this course is

Four forces are quietly rewriting the global economy: **sound money** (Bitcoin), **trust-minimized computation** (blockchains), **machine intelligence** (AI), and the **monetary system** they all push against. Most courses take one of these and ignore the other three. Most podcasts take all four and explain none of them. This course takes them seriously, together, and refuses both the cheerleading and the dismissal.

The goal is not to make you a trader, an engineer, or a prophet. The goal is to give you a working mental model — one you can defend, update, and use to read a Fed statement, a Bitcoin block, and a model release note in the same week and see them as moves in a single game.

## What this course is not

- Not a trading course. We will barely look at price charts.
- Not a coding course. You will write zero smart contracts.
- Not a futurism course. We do not predict the singularity.
- Not balanced for the sake of being balanced. The instructor has views. They are stated, defended, and open to attack.

## Who this is for

- Curious generalists who want more than headlines.
- Engineers who understand one of these four domains and want a working grasp of the others.
- Investors and operators trying to make sense of a world where capital, code, and cognition are converging.
- Anyone who has felt that the official explanation of the economy stopped making sense around 2020 and wants better tools.

## Prerequisites

None. Curiosity and a willingness to sit with uncomfortable ideas.

## How it's structured

- **8 modules**, each with 4–6 lessons.
- Each lesson: a short reading, a concrete example, and one **uncomfortable question**.
- A **mastery gate** at the end of every module — a short quiz whose explanations teach as much as the lessons. You can retake it.
- A **capstone** per module — usually a short writeup, sometimes a debate, once a recorded voice defense.
- Module-level **discussion threads** and a **study group** meeting per module.
- **Final project**: a 1,500-word personal thesis on where you think this is all going and why.

## Tone

Opinionated but grounded. If a claim is contested, it is flagged as contested. If the instructor has a position, it is stated as a position, not as fact. Steel-manning the other side is required, not optional. The point is to think more clearly, not to recruit you to a tribe.

## Module overview

1. **First Principles: What Is Money, Really?** — Before we touch a blockchain or a neural net, we agree on what money even is.
2. **Bitcoin from Scratch** — Bitcoin without the price chart. The system, not the ticker.
3. **Blockchains Beyond Bitcoin** — What is gained and lost when you generalize the idea.
4. **The Machines Arrive: A Working Model of Modern AI** — What AI actually is in 2026, free of hype and dismissal.
5. **Where AI Meets Crypto** — The most overhyped intersection in tech, with a few genuinely interesting threads.
6. **Macro: The Economy These Technologies Are Crashing Into** — The terrain being reshaped.
7. **Frictions, Failures, and Open Questions** — The honest module. Where the narratives break.
8. **Synthesis: A Personal Thesis** — Build a view you can defend.

## Grading

This is a learning course, not a credentialing course. There are no letter grades.

- **Mastery gates** are pass / retake. You pass when you can explain, not just recognize.
- **Capstones** are reviewed against published rubrics. Feedback is the point; the score is incidental.
- **Final project** is reviewed by the instructor and one peer. The bar is "could you defend this in a room of skeptics."
