# Capstone: A Quality Dashboard Critique

## The scenario

Pick a real or published quality dashboard you know well enough to critique in detail. It can be from your own institution (de-identified — see PHI rules below), from a published example (CMS Care Compare, Leapfrog Hospital Safety Grade, a state hospital association report, a vendor demo screenshot from a published case study), or from a peer-reviewed article that includes a dashboard figure. The dashboard should display at least three quality measures and should be substantial enough to support analysis through every concept in this module — a single sparkline will not do.

Write a **1,000–1,400 word critique** that walks the dashboard through measure-spec accuracy, statistical validity, frontline usability, governance, and denominator stability over time, and proposes specific redesigns where it falls short.

## What the critique must do

1. **Describe the dashboard precisely.** What does it show? Who is the intended audience (executive, operational, frontline)? What is the freshness? What decisions is it meant to support? Three to five sentences. Abstract any institutional details that would compromise confidentiality.

2. **Measure-spec literacy.** Pick at least two of the measures on the dashboard. For each, identify the numerator, denominator, exclusions, and exceptions as the spec defines them. If you cannot find the spec, that is itself a finding — note it. If the dashboard's English description does not match the underlying spec, note the discrepancy. Place each measure into Donabedian's structure-process-outcome framework.

3. **Statistical validity.** Walk the dashboard through the statistical traps from lesson 4. Is denominator drift possible? Is the sample size adequate for the conclusions the dashboard is presenting? Are the comparisons being made (across units, across time, against benchmarks) defensible, or is Simpson's paradox or selection bias plausibly contributing? If the dashboard implies that an intervention "worked," is regression to the mean a candidate alternative explanation? Be specific.

4. **Frontline usability.** Apply the Module 5 usability lens. Does the dashboard violate any Nielsen heuristics in ways that matter? Is the audience match correct, or has an executive dashboard been pushed onto frontline users (or vice versa)? Is freshness visible? Are run charts or control limits used where they should be?

5. **Governance.** Who owns the dashboard? Who decides when measures are added, retired, or redefined? If you cannot tell from the dashboard or its documentation, note that as a finding. Governance failure is the most common reason quality dashboards drift out of alignment with the institution's actual practice.

6. **Propose a multi-pronged redesign.** For each significant problem you identified, propose a specific fix. The fix should be one the institution could actually implement, not a wish list. Identify the trade-offs. Specifically address whether the dashboard should be split into multiple audience-specific views.

7. **Engage the strongest counter-argument.** The strongest counter-argument is some version of "the team that built this dashboard had reasons that were defensible at the time, the dashboard is doing operational work that the redesign would disrupt, and the cost of the redesign exceeds the value of the improvement." Steel-man it. Then respond.

## What the critique must NOT do

- Identify any specific patient. Abstract before you write. See the PHI Handling Policy.
- Identify a specific institution by name in a way that would compromise confidentiality.
- Treat the dashboard team as people who should have known better. The discipline of dashboard critique is structural; if your critique is "the analytics team should be more careful," you have not done the analysis.
- Skip the measure-spec literacy section because you "could not find the spec." If you could not find it, that is the finding — write it down and discuss what the absence of accessible spec means for the dashboard's trustworthiness.
- Be drafted by an AI. See the AI Use Policy.

## How it will be graded

By the **Written Analysis Rubric**. The "Use of frameworks and vocabulary" criterion is the heaviest hitter — graders are looking for the statistical traps, Donabedian, the audience taxonomy, and the measure-spec vocabulary doing real work, not a paragraph that names them and moves on. The "Engagement with the strongest counter-argument" criterion will weigh against you if you treat the dashboard as obviously wrong; the strongest critique acknowledges that operational dashboards are constrained artifacts and that the "right" dashboard in theory is often not the right dashboard in the room.

## Submission

Paste the critique as a single discussion post in the Module 6 capstone thread. The instructor will review against the rubric within five days. You may revise once.

## A note on what this assignment is for

Most clinical informaticists will spend more time looking at dashboards than building them. The discipline of critiquing a dashboard well — not aesthetically but analytically — is what distinguishes a board-passing informaticist from one whose presence on a quality committee actually changes what the committee decides. The analytic frameworks in this module (measure-spec literacy, the eight traps, Donabedian, the audience taxonomy) are how you walk into a meeting holding a dashboard and ask the questions that move the conversation. Practicing the critique on a real dashboard is how those frameworks become reflexes. The boards test the recognition; the job tests the discipline.
