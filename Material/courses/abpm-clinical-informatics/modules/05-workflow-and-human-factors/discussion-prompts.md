# Module 5 Discussion Prompts

Three threads. Pick at least one and post once. Reply to at least one peer on a different thread. Read the **Case Discussion Rubric** before posting — PHI hygiene is graded.

Reminder: cases are abstracted. "A 60-year-old admitted from clinic" is fine. "The patient I admitted last Tuesday" is on its way to being PHI.

---

## Thread 1 — The workaround you would defend

Pick a workaround you have observed in clinical work — a sticky note, a private spreadsheet, a paper backup, a shared document the team uses instead of the official system, a screenshot pasted into a message, a verbal handoff the EHR was supposed to replace. Describe it (abstracted) in this thread.

Then defend it. Not as a discipline failure but as an intelligent adaptation to constraints the official workflow does not acknowledge. What is the constraint? What does the workaround do that the official path doesn't? What would the institution have to change to make the workaround unnecessary, and what is the institution implicitly choosing by leaving it in place?

The point of the thread is to make you treat workarounds as design feedback and to practice the analytical move of asking "what was the user solving for" before asking "how do we get them to stop." The boards reward this move and the work rewards it more.

---

## Thread 2 — Pick a dimension and tell its story

Pick one of the eight Sittig-Singh dimensions. Tell a story (abstracted) about a time you saw that dimension contribute to a failure, a near-miss, or a project that almost succeeded. The story should be concrete enough that another reader could imagine being in the room.

Then say what surprised you about the experience. What you would want a junior informaticist to take away from the story. What you wish the field talked about more.

The point of the thread is to make the model real rather than memorized. Reading other people's stories is also one of the highest-yield ways to internalize how the dimensions actually show up — the textbook examples are too clean and the real ones are messier in instructive ways.

---

## Thread 3 — Usability vs satisfaction

The lessons distinguish usability (effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction, all three) from user satisfaction alone. In this thread, defend or attack the following claim:

> "Clinician satisfaction surveys are the wrong way to measure EHR usability, because clinicians who have learned to compensate for poor usability will report being satisfied, while clinicians who are still struggling with the same system will report being dissatisfied. The metric measures adaptation, not the system."

Take a side. Then steel-man the other side in a second paragraph. The grading is on the steel-man, not on which side you picked. The point is to surface the gap between *how the field measures usability* and *what usability actually is*, which is one of the genuine fights happening in the field right now.

## Posting expectations

- One top-level post on at least one thread.
- One reply on a different thread.
- Quote what you are responding to. "I disagree" is a 1 on the rubric.
- PHI hygiene is graded. One slip is a 1 regardless of the rest of the post.
- Substance matters more than length. A two-sentence reframe scores higher than a five-paragraph restatement.
