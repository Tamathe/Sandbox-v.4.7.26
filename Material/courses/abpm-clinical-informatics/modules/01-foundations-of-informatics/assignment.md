# Capstone: Defining the Field for a Skeptical Audience

## The scenario

You have been asked to give a 10-minute talk at your hospital's medical staff meeting on the question: *"What is clinical informatics, and why does this hospital need a CMIO?"* Your audience is a roomful of clinicians who are hostile to the EHR, distrustful of "informatics" as a buzzword, and convinced that what they really need is more nurses and fewer alerts. Several of them have asked, in writing, why this is not a job the IT director could do. The CMO has given you ten minutes and warned you that the audience will not be polite.

You will not give the talk. You will write the **800–1200 word memo you would prepare for yourself** before walking into that room. The memo is for your own use — it is the thinking that produces the talk, not the talk itself.

## What the memo must do

1. **Define clinical informatics** in a way the audience would accept. You may use AMIA's definition as a starting point but you must translate it into language a tired internist will not roll their eyes at.
2. **Distinguish clinical informatics from clinical IT** with at least one concrete example of a problem each discipline owns and one example of a problem they jointly own. The examples should be plausible at a real hospital, not abstract.
3. **Apply Friedman's Fundamental Theorem** to one specific informatics intervention (real or hypothetical) at your institution. Show the theorem doing actual work — i.e., it should change what question you ask about the intervention, not just decorate a paragraph.
4. **Engage the strongest version of the audience's objection.** The strongest objection is *not* "we don't need computers." It is some version of "informatics has been a net negative for clinician well-being and the field has not earned the trust it is asking for." Steel-man that objection in two or three sentences before responding to it. If you cannot state it in a way a hostile internist would recognize as their actual position, you have not steel-manned it.
5. **End with one concrete recommendation** for what the CMIO role at this hospital should *do first*. One thing. The kind of thing you could be held accountable for in a year.

## What the memo must NOT do

- Use the word "synergy" or any close relative.
- Cite the AMIA core competencies as if they were self-justifying. They are not. The audience does not care that a professional society exists.
- Recommend a technology purchase. The question is about the role and the discipline, not the budget.
- Outsource the writing to an AI. See the AI Use Policy. You may use a model to quiz yourself on the lesson material before you write; you may not use one to draft the memo.

## How it will be graded

By the **Written Analysis Rubric** (`rubrics/written-thesis.json`). Re-read the rubric before you start writing. The two highest-weighted criteria — accuracy and quality of reasoning — together account for more than half the score, and the steel-man criterion is weighted because the boards reward the move and the audience in the scenario will reward it more.

## Submission

Paste the memo as a single discussion post in the Module 1 capstone thread. The instructor will review against the rubric within five days. You may revise once. There is no penalty for revision; revision is the point.

## A note on what this assignment is actually for

You are ten minutes into a board prep course. The point of this capstone is *not* to test whether you have memorized lesson material — the mastery gate did that. The point is to make you produce, on paper, the version of the field's self-definition you actually believe. You will need that version for every conversation you have for the rest of your career. The boards are a small slice of that need.
