# Money, Machines, and Markets

### A course on Bitcoin, blockchains, AI, and the economy they are reshaping

---

## Course Overview

This course sits at the intersection of four forces that are quietly rewriting the rules of the global economy: **sound money** (Bitcoin), **trust-minimized computation** (blockchains), **machine intelligence** (AI), and the **macro / monetary system** they all push against. The goal is not to make you a trader, a developer, or a prophet — it is to give you a working mental model of how these systems interact, where they collide, and what an economy that runs on programmable money and programmable minds actually looks like.

The course is built to be drip-fed. Each module is self-contained but cumulative. By the end you should be able to read a Fed statement, a Bitcoin block, and a model release note in the same week and understand how all three are pulling on the same rope.

### Who this is for
- Curious generalists who want more than headlines
- Engineers who understand one of these domains and want the others
- Investors and operators trying to make sense of a world where capital, code, and cognition are converging

### Prerequisites
None. Curiosity and a willingness to sit with uncomfortable ideas.

### How it's structured
- **8 modules**, each with **4–6 lessons**
- Each lesson: a short reading, a concrete example, and one "uncomfortable question"
- Module capstones: a written reflection or a small hands-on exercise
- Final project: a 1,500-word thesis on where you think the system is going and why

---

## Module 1 — First Principles: What Is Money, Really?

Before we touch a blockchain or a neural net, we need a shared definition of the thing they are all competing to redefine.

- **1.1** A short history of money: shells, salt, silver, paper, pixels
- **1.2** The three functions: medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account — and why they don't always live in the same object
- **1.3** Fiat, credit, and the modern banking stack
- **1.4** Inflation as policy, not weather
- **1.5** What "hard" money means and why anyone cares
- **Capstone:** Write 500 words on the hardest form of money you've personally held, and why.

---

## Module 2 — Bitcoin from Scratch

Bitcoin without the price chart. We treat it as a system, not a ticker.

- **2.1** The 2008 white paper, read line by line
- **2.2** Proof-of-work, difficulty, and why electricity becomes truth
- **2.3** UTXOs, keys, and self-custody
- **2.4** Halvings, supply schedule, and the 21M cap
- **2.5** Lightning and the second layer
- **2.6** Bitcoin as a political object
- **Capstone:** Send yourself a testnet transaction and explain what each field in it means.

---

## Module 3 — Blockchains Beyond Bitcoin

What happens when you generalize the idea — and what is gained and lost in the process.

- **3.1** Ethereum and the world computer thesis
- **3.2** Smart contracts: programmable agreements and their failure modes
- **3.3** Proof-of-stake, validators, and the energy debate
- **3.4** Stablecoins: the most important crypto asset nobody talks about
- **3.5** Rollups, modularity, and the scaling wars
- **3.6** Where decentralization is real and where it is theater
- **Capstone:** Pick one non-Bitcoin chain and write a one-page honest assessment — what it does well, what it pretends to do well.

---

## Module 4 — The Machines Arrive: A Working Model of Modern AI

A grounded view of what AI is in 2026, free of both hype and dismissal.

- **4.1** From symbolic AI to deep learning to transformers — the short version
- **4.2** What a large model actually is (weights, tokens, context)
- **4.3** Training, fine-tuning, RLHF, and the cost curve
- **4.4** Agents: what changes when models can take actions
- **4.5** The compute supply chain: chips, power, water, and geopolitics
- **4.6** What models cannot do, and why that matters
- **Capstone:** Use a frontier model to perform one task end-to-end and write 300 words on where it broke.

---

## Module 5 — Where AI Meets Crypto

The most overhyped intersection in tech, with a few genuinely interesting threads.

- **5.1** The honest taxonomy: AI-on-chain, chain-for-AI, and the marketing layer in between
- **5.2** Verifiable inference and zero-knowledge ML
- **5.3** Decentralized compute and GPU markets
- **5.4** Agent wallets: when software earns, holds, and spends
- **5.5** Provenance, watermarking, and cryptographic proof of human (or not)
- **5.6** What fails when you put a probabilistic system on a deterministic ledger
- **Capstone:** Sketch the smallest useful product that genuinely needs both an LLM and a blockchain. Defend why neither alone would suffice.

---

## Module 6 — Macro: The Economy These Technologies Are Crashing Into

Step back from the tech and look at the terrain it is reshaping.

- **6.1** Sovereign debt, deficits, and the long arc of fiat
- **6.2** Energy as the real base layer
- **6.3** Labor markets when cognition is cheap
- **6.4** Capital concentration in the age of scaling laws
- **6.5** CBDCs vs. stablecoins vs. Bitcoin — three visions of digital money
- **6.6** Demographics, productivity, and the automation question
- **Capstone:** Pick one country and describe how it will likely respond to the next decade of these forces.

---

## Module 7 — Frictions, Failures, and Open Questions

The honest module. Where the narratives break.

- **7.1** Fraud, rugs, and the recurring crypto bezzle
- **7.2** Model collapse, hallucination, and the limits of synthetic data
- **7.3** Regulatory capture and the moat that is being built
- **7.4** Privacy in a world of perfect memory
- **7.5** Energy, climate, and the moral arithmetic
- **7.6** What we still genuinely don't know
- **Capstone:** Write a steel-manned critique of Bitcoin, of frontier AI, and of yourself for being interested in either.

---

## Module 8 — Synthesis: A Personal Thesis

The course exists to produce one thing: a view you can defend.

- **8.1** Building a framework: how to weigh competing narratives without becoming a zealot
- **8.2** Position sizing in ideas, not just portfolios
- **8.3** What a healthy information diet looks like in this domain
- **8.4** The next ten years: three plausible futures
- **8.5** What you owe the people you teach this to
- **Final project:** A 1,500-word personal thesis on the convergence of money, machines, and markets — what you believe, what you're uncertain about, and what would change your mind.
