# Assignment — One Honest Page on One Non-Bitcoin Chain

## The prompt

Pick one non-Bitcoin chain. Write approximately 700 words assessing it honestly across the dimensions this module gave you tools for. The chain you pick is less interesting than the assessment you produce.

You might pick:
- Ethereum (mainnet)
- Solana
- A specific L2 (Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, zkSync, Starknet, Linea, Scroll)
- A Cosmos zone
- A "high-throughput" chain like BNB Chain, Tron, or Avalanche
- Something stranger — Stacks, Mina, an appchain you have actually used

You may *not* write about Bitcoin. The point of this assignment is to apply Module 3's vocabulary to something other than the chain you spent Module 2 on.

## Required structure

Cover all five of these in some order. The proportions are up to you.

1. **What it claims to be.** In one or two sentences, what is the elevator pitch the chain's marketing actually uses? Quote the language if you can.
2. **What it actually is, in 2026.** What does the chain *do* in practice? What is the on-chain activity composed of? Where are the users? Avoid the word "ecosystem" — it is doing too much hiding.
3. **The Nakamoto coefficient (or its closest analog), as best you can estimate.** Walk through the calculation. If you cannot get a clean number, say so and explain what you would need to know to get one. The act of trying matters more than the precision of the result.
4. **The most credible critique you can make of it, steel-manned.** This is the section where students who skim will lose points. The critique cannot be a slogan ("it's centralized," "it's a VC chain," "it's too slow"). It has to be specific enough that someone defending the chain would actually have to engage with it.
5. **Your honest position.** Would you trust this chain with your own money for what kinds of activity, and not for what kinds? Be specific. "I would hold $X for Y purposes but not for Z" is the right shape of answer.

## What I'm looking for

This is graded against the **Written Thesis Rubric** (`rubrics/written-thesis.json`). The four criteria from the rubric apply here, with specific notes for this assignment:

1. **Clarity of claim** — A reader should be able to summarize your assessment in one sentence after reading. Not "it's complicated." Not "there are pros and cons." A *position*.
2. **Quality of reasoning** — Your assessment should rest on the dimensions from this module (consensus mechanism, decentralization metrics, censorship surface, trust assumptions, oracle dependence, sequencer/upgrade-key situation if applicable). Reaching outside the module's vocabulary is fine if you flag it.
3. **Engagement with the strongest counter-argument** — The maximalist case for and against this chain are both somewhere in the discourse. You should engage with the version of the counter-argument that is hardest for your position, not the easiest one.
4. **Voice and honesty** — Have you used the chain? If yes, say so and bring in what you noticed. If no, say so and lower the confidence of your conclusions accordingly. Generic chain reviews written without ever interacting with the thing are easy to spot and fail this criterion.

## What will sink it

- A 700-word press release for the chain.
- A 700-word hit piece on the chain.
- An assessment that uses "decentralized," "trustless," or "scalable" without defining what they would mean for *this specific chain*.
- Skipping the Nakamoto coefficient section because "it's hard to find the data." It is supposed to be hard. The exercise is the point.
- A comparison to Bitcoin that takes up more than two sentences. This is an assessment of the chain, not a referendum on Bitcoin.
- AI-generated prose. I will recognize it. Most generated assessments mention "Layer 2 scaling solutions" three times in the first paragraph.

## Format

- ~700 words (±150)
- Plain markdown
- A single line at the end disclosing any AI tool you used and how. ("Used Claude to check my Nakamoto coefficient estimate against current public data" is fine. "Used Claude to write the section I was bored by" is also fine, as long as you say so.)

## Why this assignment

The instinct, when learning a new technical area, is to either become a fan or become a critic, because both are easier than the third option of holding a calibrated and partially-informed view. This assignment forces you into the third option for one specific chain. If you find yourself unable to write the steel-manned critique, you have not actually learned enough about the chain yet. If you find yourself unable to write the honest position, you have not yet decided what you think — which is also fine, as long as you write that explicitly.

The first time you describe a chain you actually use in the language of "Nakamoto coefficient," "data availability assumption," and "censorship surface" and *mean it*, this module has done its job for you.
