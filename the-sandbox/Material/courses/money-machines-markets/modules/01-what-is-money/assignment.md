# Assignment — The Hardest Money You've Ever Held

## The prompt

Write 500 words on the hardest form of money you've personally held in your life, and why you would describe it that way.

"Hardest" here means whatever you want it to mean — but you have to define it, and the definition has to come out of the lessons in this module. You can use stock-to-flow. You can use store-of-value durability over your own lifetime. You can use political resistance to debasement. You can use something else, as long as you can defend it.

Your answer might be:
- The US dollar (and you make the case that despite everything, it has held up better than the alternatives in your life)
- Real estate in a specific market
- Gold coins your grandmother left you
- Shares in an index fund (controversially — is that money?)
- Bitcoin
- Frequent-flyer miles in a particular airline's program (yes, really, you can defend this one)
- Something stranger

The choice is less interesting than the defense.

## What I'm looking for

This is graded against the **Written Thesis Rubric** (`rubrics/written-thesis.json`). The four criteria are:

1. **Clarity of claim** — A reader should be able to summarize your position in one sentence after reading.
2. **Quality of reasoning** — Each step of your argument should follow from the previous one.
3. **Engagement with the strongest counter-argument** — Pick the strongest case against your choice and answer it. Not the strawman version. The real version.
4. **Voice and honesty** — I want to hear *you* in this writeup. Not a generic essay. Specific things you've held, specific moments you noticed something.

## What will sink it

- A 500-word abstract argument with no personal stake. ("The dollar is the world's reserve currency and...")
- An unhedged "Bitcoin is obviously the hardest money ever invented" with no engagement with what could make that wrong.
- An unhedged "Bitcoin is a bubble" with no engagement with what could make that wrong.
- AI-generated prose. I will recognize it. So will you when you read it back.

## Format

- 500 words (±100)
- Plain markdown
- A one-line note at the end disclosing any AI tool you used and how. ("Used Claude as a sparring partner on the counter-argument section." That's enough.)

## Why this assignment

This module spent five lessons defining money, taking the textbook story apart, and putting it back together. The point of the assignment is to force you to *use* the vocabulary on something concrete and personal. People who only think about money in the abstract will sail through this module and learn nothing. People who land the abstract concepts on a real object they have actually held will start to see the rest of the course differently.

The first time you describe something you own using the words "stock-to-flow ratio" or "monetary premium" and mean it, the course has done its job for that module.
