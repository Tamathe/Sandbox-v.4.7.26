# Module 4 — Discussion Prompts

Three threaded prompts. Pick at least one to start a thread on, and reply substantively to at least two threads started by others. Quality matters, volume doesn't. See `rubrics/discussion-quality.json` for what "substantive" means here.

---

## Prompt 1 — "Just predicting the next token"

Lesson 2 spends a lot of time saying that the mechanical description of an LLM as "a function that predicts the next token in a loop" is correct, and that the dismissive implication ("therefore it isn't really doing anything") doesn't follow. It also says the opposite framing — that next-token prediction implies real understanding — also doesn't follow.

**Question for the thread:** Take the position that you are *least* sympathetic to ("it's just statistics" or "it's already understanding"), and write the strongest defense of it you can. Then say where you think your real position actually sits and what would have to change for you to move.

The thread will get repetitive if everyone defends their actual view. The point of the exercise is to inhabit the side you don't naturally take. If you find yourself unable to write a strong version of the side you reject, that is the most useful signal you can get from this prompt.

---

## Prompt 2 — Where the bottleneck actually is

Lesson 5 argues that the binding constraint on the next five years of AI is physical infrastructure — chips, power, water, geopolitics — and that almost all of the public conversation about AI is happening in the wrong vocabulary as a result.

**Question for the thread:** Pick *one* recent piece of AI commentary you've read in the last month (a news article, a podcast, an essay, a tweet thread — anything). Quote a specific claim from it that ignores the physical layer entirely, and rewrite the claim to include the physical constraint. Does the rewritten version still make the same prediction? If not, what changed?

This prompt is concrete on purpose. If you find yourself wanting to argue in the abstract about whether the physical bottleneck "really" binds, you have not done the exercise. Pick a specific claim and a specific piece. Ground rule: no rewriting your own claims. Pick someone else's and engage with what they actually said.

---

## Prompt 3 — The cooperative-vs-autonomous gap

Lesson 4 makes the observation that the *useful* agents that have actually shipped in 2024–2026 are mostly cooperative — humans stay in the loop, the agent does the tedious parts, the human checkpoints irreversible actions — while the *marketed* agents have mostly been the fully-autonomous-AI-worker framing, which has shipped less working stuff. This is a gap between hype and product, and it has been consistent for two years now.

**Question for the thread:** Why do you think this gap exists? Is it a temporary state of the technology that will close as models get better? Is it a structural feature of the compounding-error math from the lesson? Is it a marketing dynamic where "fully autonomous" is a better fundraising story than "useful copilot"? Something else?

You don't have to commit to one explanation. But if you offer multiple, rank them by how much weight you give each, and say what evidence would shift the weights. Don't just list possibilities — that's not a position.

**Ground rule for this thread:** if you assert that "agents will [be solved / never work / replace knowledge workers]" you have to *say what specific observable change would make you wrong* in your own words. No unfalsifiable predictions.

## How to participate well

- Quote the specific thing you are responding to before responding to it.
- If you are about to type a sentence that contains a slogan ("AI is just autocomplete," "AGI is inevitable," "the AI bubble will pop"), stop and rewrite it without the slogan. The slogan is doing your thinking for you.
- It is fine and good to change your mind in public mid-thread. The discussion-quality rubric explicitly rewards this.
- It is fine to say "I don't know yet." It is not fine to perform certainty you don't feel.
- Citing benchmarks is allowed but please name *which* benchmark and *which model version*. "GPT-5 scored 87% on this" is a sentence; "AI is at 87% now" is not.
