# Academic Integrity Policy

This is a learning course, not a credential. There is no proctored exam, no GPA at stake, and nobody you can impress by gaming it. The integrity rules exist because the only person you cheat on a course like this is yourself.

## What is expected

- **Do your own thinking.** You can read anyone, talk to anyone, and use any tool. But the conclusions in your writeups have to be conclusions you actually hold and could defend out loud.
- **Cite the sources that shaped your thinking.** A footnote or an inline link is fine. You don't need a bibliography. You do need to make it possible for a reader to follow you back to where you got an idea.
- **Quote when you quote.** If a sentence is not yours, mark it as not yours. This includes sentences from AI assistants — see the AI Use Policy.
- **Engage honestly in discussion.** Steel-man the people you disagree with before criticizing them. Disagreement is welcome. Sneering is not.

## What counts as a violation

- Submitting a writeup whose ideas you cannot defend in conversation.
- Pasting someone else's words (or a model's words) into your work without marking them.
- Copying another student's mastery-gate answers.
- Using a study group to converge on a single answer everyone then submits as their own.

## Consequences

A single violation: a conversation with the instructor, the work redone.
A pattern: removal from the course. There is no appeal because there is nothing meaningful to appeal — the course was for you, and you opted out of it.

## Acknowledgment

By acknowledging this policy you are saying: *the work I submit will be work I can defend, and the words I submit will be words I either wrote or marked.*
