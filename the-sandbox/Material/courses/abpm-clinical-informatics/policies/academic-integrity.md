# Academic Integrity Policy

This is a board-prep course. The exam is proctored, the credential is real, and the only person you cheat on the practice work is yourself — because the boards don't grade on a curve, they grade on whether you know the thing.

## What is expected

- **Do your own thinking.** You can read anyone, talk to anyone, and use any tool. But the answers in your writeups have to be answers you actually understand and could defend to an examiner.
- **Cite the sources that shaped your thinking.** Inline links or footnotes are fine. The point is that a reader can trace where an idea came from.
- **Quote when you quote.** If a sentence is not yours, mark it as not yours. This includes sentences from AI assistants — see the AI Use Policy.
- **Engage honestly in case discussions.** Steel-man the clinician you disagree with before criticizing the decision. Real cases are messy and the boards reward people who can hold that messiness.

## What counts as a violation

- Submitting a writeup whose reasoning you cannot reproduce out loud.
- Pasting another source's words (or a model's words) into your work without marking them.
- Copying another candidate's mastery-gate answers.
- Using a study group to converge on a single answer everyone then submits as their own.

## Consequences

A single violation: a conversation with the instructor, the work redone.
A pattern: removal from the course. There is no appeal — the course was for you, and you opted out of it.

## Acknowledgment

By acknowledging this policy you are saying: *the work I submit will be work I can defend, and the words I submit will be words I either wrote or marked.*
