# Module 1 Discussion Prompts

Three threads. Pick at least one and post once. Reply to at least one peer on a different thread. Read the **Case Discussion Rubric** (`rubrics/discussion-quality.json`) before posting — the PHI-hygiene criterion is non-negotiable.

Reminder: you have already acknowledged the PHI Handling Policy. Abstract every case before you post. "A 60-year-old admitted from clinic with sepsis" is fine. "The patient I admitted last Tuesday from clinic" is not.

---

## Thread 1 — The IT director's question

Your hospital's CIO has asked, in a budget meeting and in writing, the following question:

> "We already have a clinical applications team inside IT. They configure the EHR, build order sets, and respond to clinician tickets. Why do we need a separate informatics function reporting to the CMO? What does the CMIO actually do that my team isn't already doing?"

Write your response as you would actually deliver it, in a budget meeting, to a CIO who is not hostile but is genuinely asking. Two to four paragraphs. Concrete examples from your experience are welcome — abstract them.

The strongest replies will be the ones that take the CIO's question at face value and answer the *real* question underneath it: where is the boundary, and how do you know which side a given problem is on?

---

## Thread 2 — A Friedman case

Pick one informatics tool you have used or seen deployed in a clinical setting. Apply Friedman's Fundamental Theorem to it, in writing, in this thread. Be specific about:

- Who is the person in the partnership?
- What is the unassisted baseline?
- What is the assisted version?
- Is the assisted version actually better, on a metric that matters? How do you know?
- If you had to evaluate this tool today with the resources of a real hospital, what would the evaluation look like?

The discipline of writing this out is the point. You will discover, when you try, that most deployments cannot answer all five questions, and that "we deployed it because the vendor said it works" is the most common substitute for an evaluation. The boards want you to recognize that gap on sight.

Reply to at least one classmate's tool and either steel-man their evaluation or push on its weakest link.

---

## Thread 3 — The DIKW pyramid, defended or buried

The lesson presents the DIKW hierarchy as a useful teaching device that becomes misleading if taken too literally. Some informaticists go further — they argue the pyramid should be retired entirely because it implies an architecture that does not match how clinical reasoning actually works. Others defend it as an essential pedagogical tool that nothing better has replaced.

Pick a side and defend it in one paragraph. Then, in a second paragraph, steel-man the other side. The grading is on the *steel-man*, not on which side you picked. The boards do not test which side is right; the field has not decided. The boards test whether you can hold both sides clearly enough to teach them.

## Posting expectations

- One top-level post on at least one thread.
- One reply on a different thread.
- Quote what you are responding to. "I disagree with Sarah" is a 1 on the rubric. "Sarah's claim that *X* is right about *Y* but I think it misses *Z*" is a 3.
- PHI hygiene is graded. One slip is a 1 on that criterion regardless of how good the rest of the post is.
- Length is not the metric. Substance is. A two-sentence post that reframes the disagreement scores higher than a five-paragraph post that restates the lesson.
