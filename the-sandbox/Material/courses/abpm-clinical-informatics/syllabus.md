# ABPM Clinical Informatics Board Prep

### A complete preparation course for the ABPM subspecialty exam in Clinical Informatics

---

## What this course is

This course exists to get you across the ABPM Clinical Informatics board exam with a real working understanding — not a stack of flashcards you forget the week after. The exam tests four domains: fundamentals of informatics, clinical decision making and care process improvement, health information systems, and leadership/change management. Most board-prep material teaches you to recognize patterns. This course teaches you to *explain* them, because the exam writers know the difference and the modern question stems reward people who can reason from first principles.

The course is opinionated about pedagogy. Each lesson ends with an uncomfortable question — something the textbooks paper over and the exam quietly tests. Each mastery gate's wrong-answer rationales teach as much as its right-answer ones, because that's how the ABPM item writers themselves are trained.

## What this course is not

- Not a question bank. Question banks are a complement, not a substitute. We will tell you which ones are good.
- Not a credential. Passing this course does not pass the boards. Doing the work in this course makes the boards pass *you*.
- Not balanced for the sake of being balanced. Where the field has reached consensus, we say so. Where it hasn't, we flag it.

## Who this is for

- Physicians preparing for the ABPM Clinical Informatics subspecialty exam.
- Fellows in ACGME-accredited Clinical Informatics fellowships who want a structured complement to their fellowship curriculum.
- Practice-pathway candidates who came up through the work and now need to formalize the vocabulary.
- Anyone in informatics leadership who wants the same mental model the boarded folks share.

## Prerequisites

- Clinical training (MD, DO, or equivalent), or equivalent clinical exposure.
- Comfort with basic statistics — sensitivity, specificity, PPV/NPV, NNT.
- No coding required. We read FHIR, we don't write servers.

## How it's structured

- **8 modules**, each aligned to a chunk of the ABPM content outline.
- Each lesson: a short reading, a concrete clinical example, and one **uncomfortable question**.
- A **mastery gate** at the end of every module — board-style MCQs and short-answer items, with rationales that teach. Unlimited retakes.
- A **capstone** per module — usually a written analysis, sometimes a discussion case, twice a recorded voice defense against a skeptical AI.
- Module-level **discussion threads** for case-based learning.
- **Study groups** for the high-volume modules (standards, analytics).
- **Final project**: design an EHR-integrated CDS module for sepsis early warning, defended in writing, voice, and a dashboard mockup.

## Module overview

1. **Foundations of Informatics** — Definitions, history, models, and the data → information → knowledge → wisdom hierarchy.
2. **Clinical Data Standards** — HL7 v2, FHIR, SNOMED CT, LOINC, RxNorm, ICD-10, CPT, UCUM. The standards exam questions love.
3. **EHR and HIS Architecture** — CPOE, CDR, the integration engine, interoperability, and what "modular" actually buys you.
4. **Clinical Decision Support** — The CDS Five Rights, alert fatigue, knowledge representation, GLIF, Arden, and modern guideline-as-service.
5. **Workflow and Human Factors** — Workflow analysis, usability heuristics, the sociotechnical model, and why the same EHR fails one clinic and succeeds in another.
6. **Quality, Safety, and Analytics** — Measure design, registries, dashboards, population health, and statistical literacy traps.
7. **Privacy, Security, and Policy** — HIPAA, HITECH, the 21st Century Cures Act, information blocking, and the security controls boards want you to know cold.
8. **Leadership and Change Management** — Project management, governance, ROI, change models, and the ethics of telling clinicians what to do.

## Grading

This is a board-prep course. The grade is whether you pass the boards.

- **Mastery gates** are pass / retake. The bar is "could you explain it to a fellow," not "did you get it right."
- **Capstones** are reviewed against published rubrics. Rubrics are at the course root.
- **Final project** is reviewed by the instructor against all three rubrics.
